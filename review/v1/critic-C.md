## Verdict summary
This is a genuinely strong speculative novel. The memory/evidence/mercy architecture is coherent and the central mechanism — a future petition that becomes executable only when present conditions are met — is elegant and well sustained. Voice work is excellent: Seven's recursive, qualification-laden narration, Iona's weary precision, Tavi's refusal to be made into evidence, and Saye's slow self-correction are distinct and credible. Pacing holds across eighteen chapters and the thematic payload (receipts are not absences; a complete record is not a complete account; a finding should not need to keep doing the harm to remain true) lands without preachiness. The defect is not in the story but in its framing apparatus: the backmatter character glossary and civic-terms list contradict the manuscript on multiple proper names and on the nature of the Ash Hearing, and invent a character and a term that appear nowhere in the text. These are real internal-consistency debts that will actively mislead a reader using the glossary. **SALVAGEABLE — findings below**
